## Execution

**A Task whose work is finished no longer stays blocked by its own duplicate
dispatch, and completion recovery no longer needs a fresh Executor run.**

- The account-owned `ControlExecutionRuntime` serializes each Task's
  snapshot/decision/application section across `TaskControlWorkflow` instances
  inside the Server `runtime.lock`. Waiting for an Executor stays outside the
  section, so draining does not hold the Task and distinct resume/recovery events
  keep their own decisions and acknowledgements.
- `queued` is no longer read as proof that a Task already yielded: queue-entry
  repair, slot convergence and periodic promotion now consult the same
  active-operation fact before reclaiming a reservation or re-serving a slot.
  Existing durable reservation/version and residue checks still govern real
  resource yield and restart recovery; this adds no lease table and no
  multi-process database writer.
- `ControlDispatchItemRepo.insertBatch()` keeps its IMMEDIATE transaction and its
  unique live-attempt constraint, but a typed, fully rolled-back live-owner
  conflict now surfaces as `DispatchAttemptConflictError`. Unknown preparation or
  database failures keep their recovery fence instead of being reclassified as a
  proved rejection.
- Startup/periodic recovery and `/task recovery` share one postcondition
  inspector over `pending` **and** `retry_authorized` items, so a legitimate
  retry is advanced by the current frontier within the existing bounded budget
  and repeated scans do not spend another authorization.
- A redundant duplicate dispatch is settled only with matching graph, generation,
  authorization, Workspace and completed-owner-receipt evidence, and only when
  its own attempts produced no effects. Its durable settlement tag keeps
  unfinished completion discoverable after a restart, including when the Task
  already reports `succeeded`.
- Completion recovery reuses certified results and the existing idempotent
  completion transaction/outbox to fill summary, actual artifacts, interaction,
  graph closure and answer delivery. Executors are not rerun, and periodic
  recovery also delivers pending completion notifications.

## Known limits

- Designed for one writable Server guarded by `runtime.lock`; multiple
  simultaneous database writers are still unsupported.
- Recovery never re-executes work to rebuild a missing result: it can only use
  results that were already certified.
